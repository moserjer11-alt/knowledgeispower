Some Essential Liturgical Norms

 

Is a Catholic priest allowed on his own initiative to add, to remove, or to change anything in the celebration of Mass? No. (cf. GIRM 24)
For adaptations and inculturation according to article 40 of the Constitution on the Sacred Liturgy, what norms are to be followed? The norms set out in the Instruction on the Roman Liturgy and Inculturation must be observed, together with those given in the General Instruction of the Roman Missal (nos. 395–399). (cf. GIRM 26)
What should the people do when the Word of God is read in the Church? The readings from the Word of God are to be listened to reverently by everyone. (cf. GIRM 29)
What is the Presiding Priest permitted to do in the Eucharistic celebration? 1. Explanation of rubrics in few words when necessary,
2. To regulate the Word of God and impart final blessings,
3. To give introduction to mass (after the initial greeting and before the penitential act),
4. Introduction to the Liturgy of the Word (before the readings), and
5. Introduction to the eucharistic prayer (before the preface), though never during the eucharistic prayer itself,
6. He may also make concluding comments regarding the entire sacred action before the Dismissal. (cf. GIRM 31)
Is a Catholic priest permitted to add his own words during the Eucharistic Prayer? No. (cf. GIRM 31)
What are the Presidential Prayers in the Eucharistic celebration? 1. The Eucharistic Prayer,
2. The Collect,
3. The Prayer over the offerings, and
4. The Prayer after communion. (cf. GIRM 30)
Can other prayers be said or the organ be played during the Presidential Prayers? No. (cf. GIRM 32)
Is singing always necessary during the celebration of the Holy Eucharist? 1. In weekdays it is not always necessary
2. But care should be taken that it is not absent during Sundays and Holydays of Obligation. (cf. GIRM 40)
Should the invocation ‘Lord, have mercy’ be said after the Penitential Act, or is it optional? Yes. The invocation ‘Lord, have mercy’ (Kyrie eleison) must be said after the Penitential Act, unless it has already been included as part of the Penitential Act itself. In that case, it is not repeated (cf. GIRM 52)
Is it permitted to use non‑biblical texts in place of the readings and the Responsorial Psalm during the Eucharistic celebration? No. (cf. GIRM 57)
From where must the readings be proclaimed during the Eucharistic celebration with the people? The readings must be proclaimed from the ambo. (cf. GIRM 58)
Is it proper for a bishop or priest who is not concelebrating to preach at that Mass? Yes, but only in particular cases and for a just cause.  (cf. GIRM 66)
Is the homily obligatory on Sundays and Holy Days of Obligation? Are there exceptions? Yes. The homily is obligatory on Sundays and Holy Days of Obligation in Masses celebrated with the people. It may be omitted only for a grave reason (cf. GIRM 66; Code of Canon Law (1983), can. 767 §2; Redemptionis Sacramentum 67).
Which are the four places in the Eucharistic celebration where the priest may use his creative ideas based on the readings? 1. During the introduction to mass (after the initial
greeting and before the penitential act),
2. During Penitential Act, (TNBCLC, GL,  2022)
2. During the Liturgy of the Word (before the readings), and
3. Before the eucharistic prayer (before the preface), though never during the eucharistic prayer itself;
4. He may also make concluding comments regarding the entire sacred action before the Dismissal. (cf. GIRM 31)
Is it permitted to use the organ and other musical instruments during the Advent season? Yes, but their use should be moderate, so as not to anticipate the full joy of the Nativity of the Lord. The character of Advent calls for restraint in musical expression (cf. GIRM 313.)
Is it permitted to use the organ and other musical instruments during the Lenten season? During Lent, the use of the organ and other musical instruments is permitted only to support the singing. Exceptions are made on Laetare Sunday, as well as on Solemnities and Feasts. (cf. GIRM 313; TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 8).
Which songs should ordinarily be chosen and sung during the Mass? Ordinarily, songs that the congregation already knows should be selected and sung, so that the faithful may participate fully and actively. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 8).
May new songs be introduced in the Eucharistic celebration? Yes, but only after the faithful have been properly trained to sing them.  (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 8)
Is it permitted to use songs from privately circulated cassettes or CDs during the Eucharistic celebration? Generally, no. Such songs are not to be used in the liturgy. However, they may be permitted if proper authorization has been obtained. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 8).
Is it permitted to sing the Gloria and the Credo using the tunes of "Suprapatham" or "Neela Kadalin Orathil"? No. These tunes must be completely avoided in the singing of the Gloria and the Credo (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 8).
What practices are to be avoided in singing during the Eucharistic celebration? The following are to be avoided in liturgical singing:
1. Choirs and musicians should not perform in an orchestral or concert style (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 8).
2. The use of track systems is to be avoided.
3. Playing music or songs from cassettes or CDs is not permitted.

Exceptionally, in children’s Masses, carefully prepared and pre recorded music may be used. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 8).
At which moments should silence be observed during the Eucharistic celebration? 1. Before penitential rite.
2. Before Collect.
3. At the end of readings.
4. At the end of homily.
5. After consuming the Eucharist.
6. Before beginning the celebrations, silence must be observed both inside and outside of the Church and also in the sacristy. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 9; GIRM 45)
Is a Catholic priest permitted to celebrate Mass wearing only a stole over casual dress, without the alb? No. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 9)
May the water to be blessed and the salt be placed upon the altar? No. (TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 9)
May the Holy Bible, which is carried in the procession, or the Lectionary, be placed upon the altar? No. It is not appropriate to place either the Holy Bible or the Lectionary on the altar (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 9).
Are both boys and girls permitted to serve at the altar? yes. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 9); Redemptionis Sacramentum 47.)
Where should the candles be placed during the Eucharistic celebration? Candles may be placed either on the altar itself or around the altar; both placements are permitted (cf. GIRM 307).
Should a cross remain on or near the altar even outside of liturgical celebrations? 1. On the altar or close to it, there is to be a cross adorned with a figure of Christ crucified (cf. GIRM 117)
2. There should be a cross, with the figure of Christ crucified, placed either on the altar or near it, and it should remain near the altar even outside liturgical celebrations. (cf. GIRM 308)
Is a cross without the figure of Christ crucified permitted for use in the Eucharistic celebration? No. A plain cross without the corpus is not permitted.(cf. GIRM 117)
If the processional cross is carried at the Entrance, where should it be placed during the Eucharistic celebration? It may be placed next to the altar to serve as the altar cross, in which case it must be the only cross used;
otherwise it is put away in a dignified place. (cf. GIRM 122)
Is it appropriate that the Most Holy Sacrament, when exposed, be left unattended even for the briefest space of time? No. It must never be let unattended. Care should be taken that at least some of the faithful are present always at fixed times. (cf. Redemptionis Sacramentum 138)
Is it permissible for a catholic priest to concelebrate  with priests or
ministers of Churches or ecclesial communities which are not in full communion with the Catholic Church?		 No. it is forbidden. (cf. Redemptionis Sacramentum 172; Code of Canon Law (1983), can. 908)
When is the Entrance Antiphon to be recited? The Entrance Antiphon is read only when there is no singing of the Entrance Hymn (cf. GIRM 48).
During the Entrance Procession, when the gesture of anjali is made, how many times should it be taken? It should be taken only once. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 10)
Where should the seat of the deacon be placed during Eucharistic celebration? The deacon’s seat should be placed near the celebrant. (cf. GIRM 310).
During the Holy Mass, may a Catholic priest conduct the introductory rites from the altar or the ambo? No. The introductory rites are to be conducted only from the priest’s chair, facing the people. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 10).
Is a Catholic priest who arrives late permitted to concelebrate? No. (cf. GIRM 206)
Is it permitted to use a secular greeting (e.g., ‘Good morning’) in place of, or following, the liturgical greeting (‘The Lord be with you’)? No. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 11)
During the Penitential Rite of the Eucharistic celebration, what gestures are prohibited for a Catholic priest when saying the absolution prayer? It is prohibited for the priest to stretch out his hands or to make the Sign of the Cross over himself. Since these gestures are not prescribed in the liturgical norms, they are to be avoided. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 11).
During the Eucharistic celebration, from where should the Liturgy of the Word be conducted? The Liturgy of the Word is to be conducted from the ambo (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 11).
From what point in the Eucharistic celebration should the altar be used? The altar is used from the Offertory until the completion of the distribution of Communion. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 11)
Which liturgical actions are to be carried out from the ambo? 1. Readings
2. Responsorial Psalm
3. The Easter Proclamation (Exultet)
4. Homily
5. Announcing the intentions of the universal prayer. (cf. GIRM 309)
Is it permitted to sing a hymn in place of the Responsorial Psalm? No, but if a Psalm has been set to music, that song may be sung in place of the responsorial Psalm. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 12)
Normally who is to read the Gospel? A deacon or a concelebrating Priest. If both are not present then the priest who says the mass, he himself will read the Gospel. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 12)
What is the proper order of the procession to the altar at the beginning of the Eucharistic celebration? a)  The thurifer carrying a smoking thurible, if incense is being used;
b)  Ministers who carry lighted candles, and between them an acolyte or other minister with the cross;
c) The acolytes and the other ministers;
d)  A reader (if there is no deacon), who may carry a Book of the Gospels (though not a Lectionary), slightly elevated;
e) The priest who is to celebrate the Mass.

If incense is being used, before the procession begins, the priest puts some into the thurible and blesses it with the Sign of the Cross without saying anything.  (cf. GIRM 120)
When a bishop is celebrating Mass and a priest is assigned to proclaim the Gospel, should that priest receive a blessing from the bishop? Yes. The priest should receive the bishop’s blessing, just as the deacon does. (cf. GIRM 212).
When a bishop presides at the Eucharistic celebration, may a deacon or priest raise and display the Book of the Gospels or impart a blessing with it? No. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 13)
With which book does the bishop bless the faithful after the proclamation of the Gospel? With the Book of the Gospels, not the Lectionary (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 13; GIRM 175).
Like the deacon, should a priest who proclaims the Gospel present it to the bishop or to the priest presiding at the Eucharistic celebration? No. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 13)
Is it permitted to deliver the homily from the altar? No. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 13)
Is it permitted to decorate the altar with flowers during Advent? Yes, but the use of flowers during Advent should be moderate (cf. GIRM 305).
Is it permitted to decorate the altar with flowers during Lenten season? No. But Laetare Sunday, Solemnities and Feasts are exceptions. (cf. GIRM 305)
When the altar is decorated, where should the flowers be placed? Flowers must be kept around the altar rather than on the altar table. (cf. GIRM 305)
Are symbolic offerings permitted during the Eucharistic Celebration? No. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 15)
Who is to sing or recite the preface? Only the principal celebrant is to sing or say the preface. (cf. GIRM 216)
During the Eucharistic Prayer, is it permitted to insert additional prayers or hymns, such as  "Arathikindrom Naangal Arathikindrom" at the time of elevation? No. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 16)
Is it permissible to compose original prayers—such as the Collect, the Prayer over the Offerings, and the Concluding Prayer—for use in Eucharistic celebrations on special occasions like a jubilee? No, this is not permitted. However, introductions to the Mass, introductions to the readings, and intercessory prayers may be composed and used. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 16)
In what manner should the concelebrants recite the consecratory prayer? They should speak in a low voice, ensuring that they do not hinder the faithful from hearing the main celebrant clearly. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 16)
Is it permitted for the faithful to join the priest in singing the doxology? No. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 16)
Is it permitted to break the Host during the consecration? No. Doing so constitutes a liturgical abuse (cf. Redemptionis Sacramentum, 55)
When should the Communion-antiphon be recited? The Communion antiphon is to be recited only when no hymn is sung during Communion.
It is recited by the priest himself after he has received communion and before he distributes communion to the faithful.(cf. GIRM 87)
Is it permitted to immerse a non‑consecrated host in the Precious Blood and then distribute it to the faithful? No. (cf. Redemptionis Sacramentum, 104
Is it permissible for extraordinary ministers of Holy Communion to independently appoint someone to distribute Communion? No. (cf. Redemptionis Sacramentum, 159)
When multiple chalices are used, is it permitted to transfer the Precious Blood from one chalice to another? No. (cf. Redemptionis Sacramentum, 106)
At what point should any announcements be made? Announcements are to be made only after the Prayer after Communion (cf. GIRM 166). At this time, practices such as garlanding bishops or releasing books or CDs should be avoided. (cf. TNBCLC, GL,  2022, p. 19)
In which season is Alleluia to be sung? The Alleluia is sung every time of year other than Lent. (cf. GIRM 62)
What is sung instead of Alleluia during Lent? "The verse before the Gospel" is sung during Lent. (cf. GIRM 62)
When may "Alleluia" or "the verse before Gospel" be omitted? When these are not sung, they may be omitted. (cf. GIRM 63)
When is the Sequence that is to be sung before Alleluia optional? The Sequence that is to be sung before Alleluia is optional except on Easter Sunday and on Pentecost Day. (cf. GIRM 64)
When there is no suitable reader present then who is to read the reading? If no other suitable reader is present, the priest celebrant should also proclaim the other readings as well. (cf. GIRM 59)
Is it permissible to replace the text of Gloria with any other? The text of this hymn may not be replaced by any other.  (cf. GIRM 53)
Who intones the Gloria hymn? It is intoned by the priest or, if appropriate, by a cantor or by the choir. (cf. GIRM 53)
In which Liturgical season is the Gloria hymn sung? It is sung or said on
1. Sundays outside advent and Lent, and
2. also on Solemnities and feasts, and
3. at particular celebrations of a more solemn character.  (cf. GIRM 53)
How many times is the acclamation (Kyrie eleison) after the penitential act  pronounced?  Each acclamation is usually pronounced twice, though it is not to be excluded that it be repeated several times, by reason of the character of the various languages, as well as of the artistry of the music or of other circumstances.(cf. GIRM 53)
When may the blessing and sprinkling of water take place? From time to time on Sundays, especially in easter time, instead of the customary penitential act, the blessing and sprinkling of water may take place as a reminder of baptism. (cf. GIRM 51)
May the introductory rites of the Mass ever be omitted? In certain celebrations that are combined with Mass according to the norms of the liturgical books, the introductory rites are omitted or take place in a particular way. (cf. GIRM 46)
Is it permissible for a lay person to deliver the homily during Eucharistic Celebration? No. (cf. GIRM 66)
Who is to intone the Creed, if it is sung? If it is sung, it is intoned by the priest or, if appropriate, by a cantor or by the choir. It is then sung either by everybody together or by the people alternating with the choir. (cf. GIRM 68)
When should the breaking of bread take place and by whom? The fraction or breaking of bread is begun after the sign of peace and is carried out with proper reverence.
This rite is reserved to the priest and the Deacon. (cf. GIRM 83)
When multiple chalices are used for Mass, must water be added to each chalice? It is sufficient to add water only to the main celebrant’s chalice. Mixing water into all the chalices, however, “would not in any way be considered to be an abuse” (cf. Committee on Divine Worship, Newsletter, May-June 2012; CDWDS, Letter, 30.4.2012.)
Is it permissible to distribute communion under two kinds on every Sunday or as wished by the Priest Celebrant? No. The following are to be noted.
In addition to those cases given in the ritual books, communion under both kinds is permitted for:
a)  Priests who are not able to celebrate or concelebrate Mass;
b)  The Deacon and others who perform some duty at the Mass;
c)  Members of communities at the conventual Mass or the “community” Mass, along with seminarians, and all those engaged in a retreat or taking part in a spiritual or pastoral gathering.
d) According to GIRM 283, Diocesan Bishops may establish norms concerning this. So these norms are also to be followed. (cf. GIRM 283)
How many candles are to be placed during the celebration of Holy Eucharist? At least two in any celebration, or even four or six, especially for a Sunday Mass or a holyday of obligation, or if the Diocesan bishop celebrates, then seven candlesticks with lighted candles. (cf. GIRM 117)
Is it appropriate that the Reader gets blessing from the priest celebrant before reading the readings? No. (cf. GIRM 128)
What should be done if host or any particle should fall on the ground? It is to be picked up reverently; and if any of the precious blood is spilled, the area where the spill occurred should be washed with water, and this water should then be poured into the sacrarium in the sacristy. (cf. GIRM 280)
Is it permissible to reserve the precious blood like that of the precious body? The Precious Blood may not be reserved, except for giving Communion to someone who is sick and is unable to consume the host. (cf. CCBI, Guideline for Extraordinary Ministers of Holy Communion, pg 6. - online edition)
For which elements three swings of the thurible are used? 1. The Most blessed Sacrament,
2. A relic of the holy cross and
3. Images of the Lord exposed for public veneration,
4. The offerings for the Sacrifice of the Mass,
5. The altar cross, the Book of the Gospels, the paschal candle, the priest, and the people. (cf. GIRM 277)
For which elements two swings of the thurible are used? For relics and images of the Saints exposed for public veneration;
(this should be done, however, only at the beginning of the celebration, following the incensation of the altar.) (cf. GIRM 277)
In what manner the altar is to be incensed? a)  If the altar is freestanding with respect to the wall, the priest incenses walking around it;
b)  If the altar is not freestanding, the priest incenses it while walking first to the right hand side, then to the left. (cf. GIRM 277)
In what manner is the Cross to be incensed? 1.The cross, if situated on the altar or near it, is incensed by the priest before he incenses the altar;
2. Otherwise, he incenses it when he passes in front of it. (cf. GIRM 277)
In what manner are the offerings to be incensed? 1. The priest incenses the offerings with three swings of the thurible
2. Or by making the Sign of the cross over the offerings with the thurible before going on to incense the cross and the altar. (cf. GIRM 277)
